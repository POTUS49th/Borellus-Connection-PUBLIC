FRIDAY, AUGUST 16, 1968

BRITISH ARCHAEOLOGIST FEARED DEAD IN TURKISH DIG RAID

Ankara blames Barzani irregulars — Rescue excavation largely destroyed FROM OUR OWN CORRESPONDENT ANKARA, AUG. 15 A British archaeologist is missing and feared dead after an armed attack upon an excavation in eastern Turkey in which seven Turkish members of the expedition were killed. The raid took place during the night of Tuesday to Wednesday at Bozuktepe, a remote mound approximately 100 miles north-east of Diyarbakır. The excavation, conducted with the cooperation of the British Institute of Archaeology at Ankara, formed part of the extensive effort to record ancient settlements which will eventually be affected by the Keban Dam reservoir. Turkish officials said that about 20 men, dressed as Kurdish irregulars and carrying automatic weapons, entered the camp shortly after midnight. Tents were set alight, stores were removed and one of the two principal trenches was destroyed by explosives. Two lorries and a light vehicle are reported missing. It is not yet known whether archaeological objects were taken with them, as a large part of the camp catalogue and several field records were destroyed in the fire. The Turkish Government has attributed the attack to men acting on the instructions of Mullah Mustafa Barzani, the Iraqi Kurdish leader. A statement issued in Ankara described the raid as a deliberate incursion by “separatist and smuggling elements” operating through the Kurdish districts of south-eastern Turkey. Gendarmerie and United State Air Force units are searching roads between Diyarbakır, Elazığ and the Iraqi frontier.
2:56 PM
The missing Briton is Dr. Austen Gilbert, who arrived at Bozuktepe only a few days before the attack to assume direction of the work. Turkish police have recovered a badly burnt body near the remains of the staff camp, but formal identification has not been made. Dr. Gilbert had been appointed following the recent death of Dr. Charles Whiteman, the former director of the excavation. Dr. Whiteman died at Bozuktepe after a prolonged period of illness, and work at the site had only lately resumed. Mr. Asil Demir, the Turkish supervisor of the labour force, was among those killed. Six workmen also died. A young Turkish archaeologist was found in Diyarbakir and is receiving treatment in the State Hospital. Officials said that she was suffering from severe shock and had so far been unable to give a coherent account of the attack. The British Institute said yesterday that Bozuktepe had produced material from several periods, including remains tentatively associated with the Seljuk and Hittite civilizations. The deeper of the two trenches had not yet been fully examined. A spokesman said: “The loss of life is our first concern. It is too early to determine how much of the archaeological record has been lost, but the damage to the excavation appears to be very considerable.” The Foreign Office is seeking further information through the British Embassy in Ankara. No decision has yet been taken on whether work at Bozuktepe can be resumed, and Turkish officials have declined to publish a full inventory of the missing stores or to permit journalists to visit the site while the security investigation continues.
